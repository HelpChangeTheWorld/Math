ADVERSARIAL REVIEW OF THE ELEVEN INPUT SAMPLING ARTICLE
20 October 2026

REALITY CHECK
The exact finite theorem appears mathematically sound. This review found no
fatal logical gap or qualifying counterexample. That is a reasoned assessment
of the argument, not a certificate of correctness or a vote by human experts.

Nothing in the supplied record establishes that the result is new, that it
resolves an established open problem, that it is important to current research,
or that it has a demonstrated practical benefit. A small mathematical result
can be correct without being a significant discovery. More AI agreement does
not answer the missing questions about prior art and significance.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY DONE
Five fresh AI sessions each examined twenty assigned questions, for one hundred
structured checks. Their roles covered algebra, the computational model,
verification software, prior literature, and public presentation. This was not
a panel of one hundred people, one hundred independent model runs, or external
peer review. Checks overlap where multiple reviewers challenge the same claim;
their ratings must not be converted into a probability that the theorem is true.

THE EXACT CLAIM THAT SURVIVED
For every set of eleven distinct, equally weighted six-bit inputs, some
deterministic two-state program that reads each bit once in a suitable fixed
order has acceptance-probability error at least 7/22 relative to all 64 inputs.
The program and its fixed reading order may depend on the selected set.
The proof permits arbitrary deterministic state transitions, including merging.

This is not a theorem that eleven observations can never estimate anything
accurately. It is not a theorem about every prescribed reading order, unequal
weights, repeated sample points, or reversible-only programs. Excluding exactly
eleven points alone does not prove a minimum of twelve; separate exclusions of
smaller sizes are needed. No twelve-point construction is established here.

THE MOST IMPORTANT OBJECTIONS
1. The headline is broader than the theorem. A more accurate title is:
   An eleven point sampling obstruction for two state programs on six bits
2. Originality and research significance are unresolved. Failure to locate an
   exact search match does not establish either one.
3. The initial eleven-point target was a question in this project. The article
   does not document that the research community regarded it as an open problem.
4. The checks are reproducible controls on parts of the reasoning and its
   implementation. They are not exhaustive enumeration of all eleven-point
   supports or a formal proof-assistant certificate.
5. Some checking helpers rely on caller-supplied structural assumptions and
   Python assertions. Their ordinary generated programs passed the structural
   checks, but their API is not a hardened independent certificate checker.
6. For public reproducibility, the blog must attach or link the evidence ZIP.
   The current draft mentions the package without inventing a public URL.

A CONCRETE ADDITION FROM THE REVIEW
The example in the article has a stronger failing program than the displayed
balanced witness. With x1 the most significant bit, let

g(x) = x6 AND (x5 OR ((NOT x1) AND x2 AND x4)).

Read x1,x2,x3,x4,x5,x6 in that order, ignoring x3. The state updates are
q = NOT x1; q = q AND x2; no change; q = q AND x4;
q = q OR x5; q = q AND x6. Accept state one.

On S = {15,21,23,27,29,30,31,38,43,56,63}, it accepts eight points:
{15,21,23,27,29,31,43,63}. It accepts eighteen of all sixty-four cube points.
Its discrepancy is 8/11 - 18/64 = 157/352, about 44.60 percentage points.
The coordinating session independently checked this formula and program on all
64 inputs. This does not contradict the theorem, which supplies a lower bound.
It shows why the article's 31.82-point example is not evidence of full-class
optimality. Neither calculation determines the best possible sampler.

PUBLICATION JUDGMENT
The defensible presentation is an openly reviewable, AI-assisted research note
about a narrow finite obstruction. Narrow the headline, preserve the exact
assumptions and AI disclosure, and attach the reproducible evidence. Do not
describe it as a verified original breakthrough or a world-changing discovery.
The stated ambition to improve the world is a motivation, not a demonstrated
consequence of this theorem.

The next useful scrutiny is from a human researcher familiar with pseudorandomness
or coding theory, especially on whether the argument is already known and whether
it contributes anything beyond an instructive finite example. No such researcher
was contacted or represented as endorsing the work during this audit.

ALGEBRA
AI algebra audit

001 | A01 | PASS
Does the affine-versus-quadratic case split cover the exact quantifiers and error threshold in the stated theorem?
For an affine function with nonzero linear part, true acceptance is 1/2. Sample weights 0, 1, 2, 9, 10, or 11 give discrepancy at least 1/2 - 2/11 = 7/22. Complementation converts a high sample weight into a low one. Otherwise every nonconstant affine weight lies in 3 through 8, exactly the premise used subsequently. The construction chooses its coordinate order after S is fixed, consistent with the stated quantifier order.

002 | A02 | PASS
Are all nonconstant affine tests genuinely balanced and computable in the specified two-state model?
For a coefficient a_k = 1, the involution x -> x + e_k exchanges the two affine outputs and has no fixed points, giving 32 accepted cube inputs. Initialize q to zero and apply q <- q XOR (a_k x_k) while reading each coordinate once; add the constant in a transition or the acceptance rule. Ignoring a coordinate is an allowed identity transition.

003 | A03 | PASS
Could evaluation fail to be injective, invalidating the asserted seven-dimensional code?
The vector space of affine polynomials has dimension seven. A nonzero element in the evaluation kernel cannot be the constant one polynomial, since its evaluation has eleven ones. It would therefore be nonconstant with sample weight zero, contradicting the remaining-case hypothesis. Thus the evaluation map has zero kernel and its image has dimension seven.

004 | A04 | PASS
Does the claimed minimum weight cover every nonzero codeword, including constants?
Every nonzero affine polynomial is either the constant one, of evaluation weight eleven, or nonconstant, of evaluation weight between three and eight under the current hypothesis. Consequently every nonzero vector in C has weight at least three. The zero constant is the only zero codeword by injectivity.

005 | A05 | PASS
Is a rank-four parity-check matrix with precisely this kernel guaranteed to exist?
Since C has dimension seven inside F2^11, its annihilator under the standard dot product has dimension four. Taking a basis of that annihilator as the rows of H gives rank four, C contained in ker H, and dim ker H = 11 - 4 = 7. Equality of the two seven-dimensional spaces follows. No additional coding-theory classification is needed.

006 | A06 | PASS
Can H have zero or repeated columns despite the code's minimum-weight statement?
If column k is zero, H e_k = 0 puts a weight-one vector in C. If columns k and l coincide for distinct indices, H(e_k + e_l) = 0 puts a weight-two vector in C. Both violate A04. Hence H's columns are eleven distinct members of the fifteen nonzero vectors in F2^4.

007 | A07 | PASS
Is the claim of seven unordered pairs summing to a nonzero syndrome exact?
Fix s != 0. On F2^4 excluding 0 and s, the map a -> a + s is a fixed-point-free involution on fourteen elements. It therefore forms exactly seven disjoint pairs {a, a+s}. Both entries are nonzero, distinct, and sum to s. Neither 0 nor s participates.

008 | A08 | PASS
Can deleting four columns leave fewer than four distinct weight-at-most-two representatives of a nonzero syndrome?
If s is retained, each of the four deleted vectors destroys at most one of A07's seven disjoint pairs, leaving at least three pairs plus the singleton s. If s is omitted, its deletion destroys no pair and the other three deletions leave at least four pairs. Distinct surviving pairs produce distinct weight-two vectors. Independent exhaustive enumeration of all C(15,11) times 15 = 20,475 cases gave representative counts four in 12,600 cases, five in 7,560 cases, and six in 315 cases; none had fewer than four.

009 | A09 | PASS
Does containment of every coordinate pair product really force the entire affine evaluation code to be multiplicatively closed?
Write arbitrary codewords as alpha_0*1 + sum alpha_i v_i and beta_0*1 + sum beta_j v_j. Expanding their coordinatewise product gives a linear combination of 1, the v_i, and the v_i v_j. The diagonal products satisfy v_i^2 = v_i because entries are bits. Thus the hypothesized off-diagonal product containment, together with the existing generators, contains every expanded term and their sum.

010 | A10 | PASS
Does the point-indicator argument actually force dimension eleven, or does it assume interpolation without proof?
For t in S, each factor x_i + 1 + t_i equals one exactly when x_i = t_i. Their six-factor product is therefore the indicator of t on the full cube. Each factor evaluates into C, and multiplicative closure would place its product in C. Distinct sample points make these eleven evaluations the eleven separate standard basis vectors of F2^11. Their linear independence contradicts dim C = 7. Distinctness is explicitly part of the theorem.

011 | A11 | PASS
Does the failed closure yield a distinct-coordinate product outside C and transfer the syndrome count to its affine coset?
By A09-A10 at least one v_i v_j is outside C; it cannot have i = j because v_i^2 = v_i is already in C. For h = v_i v_j, Hh is nonzero. Moreover Hy = Hh if and only if y+h is in ker H = C, so the complete syndrome fiber is exactly h+C. The at-least-four representatives counted in A08 therefore belong to the required coset.

012 | A12 | PASS
Could a missing pair cell undermine the argument about corrections involving other coordinates?
For a pair value (u,v), its indicator is (x_i+1+u)(x_j+1+v), which expands as x_i x_j plus an affine polynomial in x_i and x_j. If the cell were empty on S, its evaluation would be zero, implying h belongs to span{1,v_i,v_j}, hence to C. That contradicts the chosen h. All four pair cells must therefore be nonempty.

013 | A13 | PASS
Are all eight elements of h+L exactly single-cell or three-cell indicators, with no overlooked type?
On the four pair inputs, the XOR of the four outputs of xy+ax+by+c is one, because the degree-two coefficient is one. Thus its truth table has odd weight: one or three. The eight coefficient triples (a,b,c) give distinct truth tables and exhaust the four singleton indicators and their four complements. Since all pair cells occur on S, their evaluations remain distinct. Independent enumeration of all eight truth tables confirmed four of weight one and four of weight three.

014 | A14 | PASS
Can h+L contain four low-weight vectors despite having only four nonempty pair cells?
Every three-cell indicator has sample weight at least three because each cell is nonempty. Hence a vector of weight at most two in h+L must indicate a single cell of size one or two. If all four cells had size at most two, S would have at most eight elements, contradicting its size eleven. Thus there are at most three such vectors. The count compares distinct evaluation vectors, not merely polynomial expressions.

015 | A15 | PASS
Does the counting difference really force an affine correction using an outside coordinate?
The coset h+C has at least four low-weight vectors, while its subset h+L has at most three. Choose y in the set difference and put p = y+h. Then p is in C but not L. Injectivity of affine evaluation supplies a unique affine polynomial for p. If that polynomial used only x_i, x_j, and a constant, its evaluation would lie in L. Therefore at least one other coordinate has coefficient one.

016 | A16 | PASS
Can the selected quadratic fail to be balanced, invalidating its claimed discrepancy?
Choose k outside {i,j} with coefficient one in p. Flipping x_k leaves the product x_i x_j unchanged and flips p, so it flips f = x_i x_j + p. The cube partitions into 32 opposite-output pairs, giving acceptance exactly 1/2. Its evaluation is y, of weight at most two, so its discrepancy is at least 1/2 - 2/11 = 7/22. Finally 7/22 - 1/4 = 3/44 > 0, establishing the stated quarter-error impossibility.

017 | A17 | PASS
Is the quadratic identity and its two-state realization correct for every coefficient choice, including constants and ignored coordinates?
Expanding (x_i+a_j)(x_j+a_i) gives x_i x_j + a_i x_i + a_j x_j + a_i a_j. Adding b+a_i a_j cancels the extra product constant. Store x_i+a_j, AND with x_j+a_i, then XOR the selected remaining bits and the final constant. Each update uses only the current state and current input, and every coordinate is read once; overwrite and AND transitions are legal in this model. Independent evaluation of all 1,800 balanced coefficient/constant formulas on all 64 inputs produced 115,200 pointwise comparisons with no implementation disagreement and no unbalanced formula.

018 | A18 | PASS
Is the claimed family of 963 balanced tests sufficient, correctly counted, and free of accidental duplicates?
There are 2^6-1 = 63 nonzero linear parities. For each of C(6,2)=15 quadratic pairs, the outside four coefficients have 15 nonzero assignments and the inside two have four assignments, giving 60 choices and 900 quadratics. Different algebraic normal forms give different Boolean functions. Complementing a function preserves absolute discrepancy, so constant terms may be dropped for this claim, although doing so can change low sample weight into high sample weight. Independent enumeration found exactly 963 distinct truth tables, all with 32 ones.

019 | A19 | PASS
Does the published numerical example work under its explicitly stated bit convention?
Using x_1 as the most significant bit and x_6 as the least significant bit, direct evaluation of x_5 x_6 + x_4 + x_5 + x_6 on the eleven listed integers accepts exactly 27 and 43, and accepts 32 of all 64 integers. The displayed first two transitions produce (1+x_6)(1+x_5); the third XORs x_4+1, yielding exactly the stated polynomial. The discrepancy is therefore exactly 7/22 for this particular function and sample.

020 | A20 | LIMITATION
Does the equality example establish that 7/22 is the best possible worst-case discrepancy for the whole program class?
No. On the same displayed sample, the allowed program g(x) = x_6 AND (x_5 OR ((1+x_1) AND x_2 AND x_4)), with x_3 ignored, accepts eight sample points and eighteen cube points. Its discrepancy is 8/11 - 18/64 = 157/352, approximately 0.446023, strictly above 7/22. It is realized in order x_1,...,x_6 by q <- 1+x_1; q <- q AND x_2; ignore x_3; q <- q AND x_4; q <- q OR x_5; q <- q AND x_6. Independent enumeration of all 18,664 functions in that fixed order confirmed this larger discrepancy. The article explicitly disclaims an exact optimum, so this is a boundary on interpretation rather than a failure of its stated theorem.

Reviewer assessment
The exact stated theorem passes this adversarial algebra audit: for every eleven-element subset of F2^6 with equal weights, an allowed deterministic two-state read-once program, with its order chosen for that subset, has discrepancy at least 7/22 and hence greater than 1/4. The affine-code, syndrome-counting, multiplicative-closure, pair-cell, balancing, and implementation steps all withstand independent re-derivation. No substantive proof gap or counterexample to that theorem was found. This is an AI audit, not independent human endorsement. Fresh exhaustive finite checks covered 20,475 syndrome cases, eight pair-cell truth tables, all 1,800 balanced quadratic implementations on 64 inputs, and the displayed example; they did not enumerate all eleven-element cube subsets. Universality rests on the analytic proof. The result is a lower bound, not an exact optimum for the full program class; the displayed sample itself admits the stronger discrepancy 157/352. No conclusion about twelve-point sufficiency, nonuniform sampling, prescribed arbitrary orders, reversible-only programs, or novelty follows from this audit.


MODEL
Fresh AI review of the computational model and quantifiers; one AI session performing 20 structured checks, not 20 independent mathematicians

021 | M01 | PASS
Is the target distribution precisely specified, rather than an unstated population or input distribution?
The model defines U as uniform on all 64 elements of F_2^6. Every true acceptance probability and balancing argument is relative to this distribution. The theorem makes no unsupported claim for an arbitrary real-world distribution.

022 | M02 | LIMITATION
Can the result be transferred directly from eleven distinct support points to eleven draws with repetitions?
No. S is explicitly an eleven-element subset, with eleven distinct points. Repeated draws produce a weighted distribution on a smaller support. Step three uses distinctness to make each point indicator evaluate to a separate unit vector; this argument cannot simply be reused on repeated list entries. The appendix expressly excludes repeated sample points, but the standalone headline does not convey this restriction.

023 | M03 | LIMITATION
Does the proof rule out an arbitrary distribution supported on eleven points?
No. The proof turns a Boolean vector of weight at most two into acceptance probability at most 2/11, which requires equal masses. With nonuniform masses, two accepted support points might carry much more mass. Nothing here rules out an eleven-point distribution with optimized unequal weights. The theorem and unresolved-questions section correctly state this exclusion.

024 | M04 | PASS
Does the text state the correct adversarial order of quantifiers?
The text explicitly states: for every S, there exists a program and a fixed reading order, both allowed to depend on S. This is exactly the quantifier order needed to disprove a single fixed distribution fooling every program. It is not a statement that the witness must be chosen before the sample.

025 | M05 | PASS
Could a reader incorrectly infer that one universal balanced program defeats every eleven-point sample?
The text explicitly says the failing program may depend on the sample. This distinction is necessary: for any fixed balanced Boolean program, choose five accepted inputs and six rejected inputs. Its sample error is then |5/11 - 1/2| = 1/22, far below 7/22. Thus a single universal balanced witness is impossible, and the article does not claim one.

026 | M06 | LIMITATION
Does the construction prove the same lower bound for every coordinate order fixed in advance?
No. The constructed witness reads its selected product coordinates first, then the remaining coordinates. The proof therefore establishes a result for the union of the program classes over all fixed permutations. It does not establish the stronger quantifier order: for each prescribed permutation and each S, a witness exists in that order. The appendix explicitly acknowledges this point.

027 | M07 | PASS
Does choosing the reading order after seeing S secretly introduce input-adaptive queries?
No. S selects the witness program before that program is run on an input x. Within each execution the six-coordinate permutation is fixed and does not depend on the values read. This is an oblivious read-once program, even though the adversary selects which such program to use against S.

028 | M08 | LIMITATION
Does 'only two internal states' mean an autonomous two-state machine with a single transition rule and no externally specified position?
No. The formal model is a layered width-two program: the input order and six transition functions are part of its fixed description, and the transition function may depend on the layer. Two states measure the state carried between layers, not the complete program description or all control needed by a physical implementation. The ordinary-language section does specify step-dependent rules, but 'very small program' is not a formal program-size bound beyond this model.

029 | M09 | LIMITATION
Is arbitrary deterministic state merging genuinely allowed, and can the result be asserted for reversible transitions?
State merging is explicitly allowed in the model, so the AND update is legal. For an input bit making its second factor zero, this update sends both prior states to zero and is noninvertible. The proof consequently does not establish the claim for reversible or permutation transitions. Both the model and the example disclose this limitation.

030 | M10 | PASS
Can every proposed pair-product witness actually be computed with two states under the stated rules?
I independently enumerated all 15 coordinate pairs, all 60 affine coefficient vectors having a nonzero coefficient outside the pair, and both constants. For all 1,800 formulas and all 64 inputs, the pair-first state updates agreed with the formula. The identity (x_i+a_j)(x_j+a_i) + r + b + a_i*a_j expands to x_i*x_j + a_i*x_i + a_j*x_j + r + b over F_2. No additional stored input bit is needed.

031 | M11 | PASS
Does the construction require an unmentioned choice of initial state or a more powerful acceptance rule?
It does not. Starting at state zero, the first update stores x_i+a_j. The final constant b+a_i*a_j can be XORed into the last of the four remaining transitions, after which acceptance is simply final state one. I used exactly this convention in the exhaustive 1,800-formula implementation check.

032 | M12 | PASS
Are irrelevant coordinates actually read once, and does ignoring their value violate the model?
The construction schedules every coordinate exactly once. A coordinate with coefficient zero uses the identity transition on the state for either input bit. Reading a variable does not require the computed function to depend on it, and the stated arbitrary-transition model imposes no such requirement. This also makes ordinary parity functions legal six-step programs.

033 | M13 | PASS
Is the asserted true acceptance rate of exactly one half justified for the nonlinear witnesses?
Step four selects an affine correction using at least one coordinate outside the product pair. Toggling that outside coordinate toggles the output and leaves the product unchanged, pairing the 64 cube points into opposite-output pairs. Independently generated truth tables for all 963 stated functions each contained exactly 32 ones.

034 | M14 | PASS
Does the numerical discrepancy really violate the inclusive error allowance, without a bias-convention error?
The quantity is absolute acceptance-probability error: 1/2 - 2/11 = 7/22, and 7/22 - 1/4 = 3/44 > 0. This is 31.818... percentage points, exceeding 25 points by 6.818... points. A signed parity bias would be twice this number; the article explicitly distinguishes that convention. I also checked that the displayed example accepts exactly sample points 27 and 43 and exactly 32 full-cube points.

035 | M15 | PASS
Are there really 963 distinct balanced tests in the proposed finite witness family?
There are 63 nonzero linear parities. For each of the 15 unordered coordinate pairs, 64 minus 4 equals 60 linear coefficient vectors use an outside coordinate, giving 900 quadratics. I generated every truth table and found exactly 963 distinct tables, all balanced. Distinct quadratic monomials also distinguish pairs in the unique Boolean multilinear representation.

036 | M16 | PASS
Is failure against this small test family logically sufficient for failure against the full program class?
Yes. Every member of the 963-function family has an allowed program implementation, so the family is a subclass. An absolute-error witness in this family defeats the larger class. The article correctly avoids the converse: these 963 functions do not represent every allowed program, and passing them on some other distribution would not by itself prove fooling of the full class.

037 | M17 | LIMITATION
After constants are removed, does the 963-function family still guarantee a witness with sample rate at most 2/11?
It guarantees the absolute discrepancy, but the selected constant-free member may have sample rate at least 9/11 rather than at most 2/11. Complementing it recovers the low-rate witness and remains legal in the full program class. The finite-family section correctly invokes preservation of absolute discrepancy; its 963 count should not be restated as 963 tests that always supply the low-tail witness themselves.

038 | M18 | LIMITATION
Does this theorem imply that a random eleven-input sample fails to estimate a fixed program with high probability?
No. For each realized eleven-point subset there is some witness, and that witness may vary with the realized subset. This is a simultaneous guarantee over the class, not a probability estimate for one program fixed independently of the sample. The ordinary-language section makes this distinction explicitly. The reported seeded random-check frequencies supply no contrary probabilistic theorem.

039 | M19 | LIMITATION
Does ruling out exactly eleven equal-weight points prove a lower bound of twelve on the optimal support size?
Not by itself. The theorem excludes exactly eleven distinct equally weighted points. Padding a smaller sample to eleven changes all its weights, so a smaller feasible equal-weight sample would not automatically yield an eleven-point one. Separate arguments are required for every smaller size, and twelve-point feasibility is also unproved here. The article expressly limits its self-contained theorem accordingly.

040 | M20 | PASS
Is the existential witness operationally obtainable, and do the finite checks replace the universal proof?
A concrete constructor can enumerate the fixed 963-family truth tables, evaluate them on S, choose a failing member, and complement if necessary. The proof establishes that such a search succeeds for every eligible S; the explicit pair-first construction then supplies a program. My exhaustive formula checks verify representation and balancing, not all eleven-subsets of the cube. The article accurately reserves universality for the analytic argument rather than claiming its sampled experiments exhaust all supports.
