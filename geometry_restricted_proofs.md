# Geometry/algebra work round: derivations and scope

These are bounded computations and restricted arguments for items 56–80. They are not solutions of the full open problems, and no novelty claim is made. Run `python3 geometry_calculations.py` from the extracted archive directory to reproduce all numerical and discrete certificates. The script uses only Python's standard library. The independent finite interpolation certificates are in `geometry_interpolation_certificates.json`.

## 56–59: explicit elementary controls

For item 56, on the standard sphere in R5 the height function is stationary only where its gradient is normal to the sphere, namely at the two poles. Pole charts give h±(x)=±sqrt(1−|x|²), with Hessians ∓I4 at zero. Thus its two critical points have indices 4 and 0. This is a standard-sphere certificate only, not a simplification of any proposed exotic sphere.

For item 57, put r=|y|². The maps Φ±(y)=(2y/(1+r), ±(1−r)/(1+r)) from the closed unit ball to the matching closed hemispheres satisfy the sphere equation because 4r+(1−r)²=(1+r)². The inverses are y=x/(1±t), with denominators at least one on the relevant hemisphere. These are smooth maps of manifolds with boundary and identify the common boundary with the equator. No general embedding is straightened.

For item 58, words are encoded by signed integers ±1,±2. The executable certificate applies only relator inversion, right multiplication by another relator or its inverse, and conjugation by a generator word. Eight moves were used to construct the input from (x,y); the inverse moves are saved and were replayed exactly. The input is therefore a calibration by construction, not an independent test of a hard candidate.

For item 59, the properly embedded standard disk (u,v,0,0) has radial square u²+v², a single interior minimum, and no interior maxima. This explicitly certifies a ribbon disk for the unknot only.

## 60–63: knot and four-manifold diagnostics

For item 60, the trefoil Jones polynomial in one conventional orientation is t+t³−t⁴. Multiplicativity under connected sum and exact convolution give t²+2t⁴−2t⁵+t⁶−2t⁷+t⁸. Its breadth is six. For a knot diagram with c crossings, the Kauffman bracket extreme-state estimate gives bracket span at most 4c; writhe normalization does not change span, and t=A^−4 divides span by four. Consequently Jones breadth is at most crossing number. The standard joined diagram has six crossings, proving c(T#T)=6. The breadth bound does not universally attain crossing number. See the primary [span discussion](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.5374).

For item 61, the knot exterior has H1 generated by its meridian μ and the preferred longitude λ has zero class. Filling along pμ+qλ therefore imposes pμ=0, yielding Z/pZ, with p=0 interpreted as Z. The full reduced slope set in the executable is finite and unambiguous; its 198 rejected pairs have different absolute numerators. The remaining 55 pairs have only survived a necessary test.

For item 62, substituting q=exp(2πi/N) in equation (16) of the primary [colored-Jones study](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18575v1) gives q^−Nk=1 and

`(1−q^j)(1−q^−j)=4 sin²(πj/N)`.

This yields the positive sum/product expression used in the executable. Pi is computed by Machin's arctangent formula, sine by its Taylor series, and logarithms by Decimal's implementation. Runs at 70 and 130 digits agreed within 10^−65 for the three requested N values. This is not directed-rounding interval arithmetic and does not certify a convergence rate. The source supplies the normalization and benchmark volume; the computed finite values are our own evaluations.

For item 63, the form 2k(−E8)⊕lH has rank 16k+2l and signature −16k. Substitution converts the 11/8 target into l≥3k and the older Furuta 10/8+2 inequality into l≥2k+1. Summing the intervening integer counts for k=1,…,8 gives 0+1+…+7=28. This is not a list of possible smooth forms: stronger known bounds exist, including the results in the [cited primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04052).

## 64–67: actual restricted algebraic proofs

For item 64, a lift F of f:Tn→Tn inducing A on the fundamental group satisfies F(x+z)=F(x)+Az. If f is a homotopy equivalence, A lies in GL(n,Z). Then H_s(x)=(1−s)F(x)+sAx has the same equivariance and descends to a homotopy from f to the linear homeomorphism A. The five-by-five integral example and its exact inverse are saved. This proves the torus-to-torus assertion; it does not identify an arbitrary source manifold with a torus.

For item 65, a surface has L-class 1 because all positive L-components have degrees divisible by four. Only a degree-two class a contributes to the higher signature, and naturality gives ⟨h*f*a,[N]⟩=⟨f*a,h_*[N]⟩. An orientation-preserving homotopy equivalence sends [N] to [M]. This proves the equality in dimension two only.

For item 66, the infinite dihedral group has multiplication (n,ε)(m,δ)=(n+(−1)^εm,ε+δ mod2). Its integer subgroup has index two. Since the whole group is virtually cyclic, a point satisfies the fixed-point requirements for E_VCyc(G). The assembly is consequently the identity. No assertion about a group outside that family follows.

For item 67, Eisenstein's criterion at 2 proves x³−2 irreducible. The discriminant formula −4a³−27b² gives −108, not a square in Q. A transitive subgroup of S3 is A3 or S3, and the nonsquare discriminant excludes A3. Thus its splitting field realizes S3 over Q.

## 68: split quadric divisor classes

The Segre parametrization identifies Q:x0x1−x2x3=0 over F7 with P1×P1. The enumeration normalizes the first nonzero homogeneous coordinate to one, checking all 400 points of P3(F7) and finding 64 on Q. The product description independently gives (7+1)² points.

The two rulings are curves defined over F7. Each has self-intersection zero and they meet once, giving determinant −1. Their classes are therefore linearly independent. Künneth gives H²(Qbar,Qℓ(1)) dimension two for ℓ≠7, so the ruling classes form a basis. Because these cycles are defined over F7, the entire basis is Frobenius fixed. This proves the divisor Tate statement for this quadric with Qℓ coefficients. Merely counting points on an unrelated surface would not prove its cycle statement.

## 69: algebraic inverse Lefschetz operators on E×E

Let E be the smooth complex elliptic curve y²=x³−x and X=E×E. Write a_i,b_i for a symplectic basis of H¹ on factor i and ω_i=a_i∧b_i, normalized by integral one. The product polarization is h=ω_1+ω_2. Use bases

`H¹: (a1,b1,a2,b2)`

`H³: (ω1∧a2,ω1∧b2,a1∧ω2,b1∧ω2)`.

Exterior multiplication by h has columns (e3,e4,e1,e2), a permutation whose square is identity. This is the saved matrix computation. To supply algebraicity rather than only a numerical inverse, let C1 be the correspondence which integrates on the first E factor and is the identity on the second. Its support in X×X is, after the natural permutation of factors, `(E×E)×Δ_E`. Define C2 symmetrically. These are algebraic cycles of codimension one.

C1 sends ω1∧a2 to a2 and ω1∧b2 to b2, and kills the other H³ basis vectors. C2 sends a1∧ω2 to a1 and b1∧ω2 to b1, and kills the first two. Hence C1+C2 is precisely the inverse of L:H¹→H³. Also h²=2ω1ω2, so (1/2)C1C2 inverts L²:H⁰→H⁴. The diagonal gives the identity in the middle degree. Thus all inverse hard-Lefschetz maps required here are induced by rational algebraic correspondences. This is the complex, product-polarized example only.

## 70: canonical ring counts on a product fourfold

Take the smooth completions C:y²=x⁵−1 and E:y²=x³−x over C. The defining polynomials are squarefree. Their double covers of P1 have six and four branch points respectively, so Riemann–Hurwitz gives g(C)=2 and g(E)=1.

For X=C×C×E×E, the canonical bundle is the external tensor product of the canonical bundles of its factors. Since K_E is trivial, K_X is the pullback of K_(C×C). The genus-two canonical bundle is ample and globally generated; hence so is its external tensor product on C×C. Its pullback is globally generated and nef, proving semiampleness for this explicit fourfold.

For m≥2, Riemann–Roch on C gives

`h⁰(C,mK_C)−h⁰(C,(1−m)K_C)=deg(mK_C)+1−g=2m−1`.

The second term vanishes because its degree is negative. On E, h⁰(E,mK_E)=1. Künneth therefore gives h⁰(X,mK_X)=(2m−1)². At m=1 the count is 2·2·1·1=4, and at m=0 it is one. These yield the exact saved counts 1,4,9,25,49,81,121.

The pulled-back canonical divisor has nonzero square but zero cube, with positive intersection of its square against a product ample class squared. Hence its numerical dimension is two. At m=1, the canonical map on each C is its degree-two hyperelliptic map: the product canonical map is not an embedding of C×C. For m≥3, the pluricanonical maps on C are embeddings, so the product pluricanonical map has the expected E×E fibers. Nothing here establishes semiampleness for a non-product variety.

## 71: displayed failure of a termination inference

The recurrence x_(n+1)=x_n/(1+x_n), x_0=1, has exact solution x_n=1/(n+1), proved by substitution and induction. It is strictly decreasing and positive for every finite n. Thus a proposed proof using only strict decrease of a nonnegative real quantity has a logical gap: its value set need not be well founded. These numbers are not constructed geometric flips or discrepancies.

## 72–73: characteristic-zero implications of the finite matrices

The ten fixed integer points are P_i=(i,(i²+i⁵) mod101), i=0,…,9. The second coordinate is an ordinary integer residue, not a reduction of the eventual characteristic-zero field. A degree-d affine polynomial represents a homogeneous degree-d section of O(d) on P2 in the chart z=1. Vanishing to order m is equivalent to vanishing of all partial derivatives ∂x^u∂y^v with u+v<m. The script computes these integer entries exactly.

A nonzero minor modulo 1009 is a nonzero integer and hence is also nonzero over Q and C. For item 72, full-column ranks 10,28,55 at (d,m)=(3,1),(6,2),(9,3) prove that no nonzero polynomial satisfies those jets at this particular complex configuration. All lower degrees are included in the tested polynomial space. Nonvanishing of the same minor defines a nonempty Zariski-open set of configurations, so the same statement holds for very general ten points. This proves precisely those three multiplicities, not a bound for unbounded m.

For item 73, the first three matrices have full row ranks 12,14,20 and column counts 15,15,21, respectively. Consequently their vector-space kernel dimensions over characteristic zero are 3,1,1; their projective dimensions are 2,0,0. Again, full rank holds on a nonempty open set. The special control uses degree two and only two double points, with eight zero multiplicities. The two points are (0,0),(1,2), and (y−2x)² is an exact nonzero kernel element. A nonzero 5×5 minor certifies rank at least five; the witness proves rank at most five, so the characteristic-zero kernel is exactly one-dimensional. On the blowup, E=H−E1−E2 is the line through the two points and has square −1. The tested divisor is D=2E, with D·E=−2. This explicitly exhibits the familiar doubled-line special mechanism.

## 74: six-dimensional constant-curvature Euler density

In a positively oriented orthonormal coframe, a constant-sectional-curvature metric has curvature forms Ω_ij=κθ_i∧θ_j. Every one of the 15 perfect matchings in the six-dimensional Pfaffian contributes +κ³ times the volume form: the permutation sign is canceled by the reordering sign of the exterior product. Thus Pf(Ω)=15κ³ dvol and the Euler form is `(2π)^−3 Pf(Ω)`. Gauss–Bonnet gives a positive Euler characteristic when κ>0. For the unit S6, the exact integral is `(15/(8π³))(16π³/15)=2`. Variable sectional curvature cannot be substituted for this constant κ.

## 75: all conformal factors on the standard product are excluded

Let g0 be the standard product metric on S2×S2, and let g~=e^(2f)g0 for any smooth f. Compactness supplies a global minimum p of f. At p, df=0 and Hess_0 f is positive semidefinite. Choose g0-unit vectors X and Y tangent to the two distinct factors. The mixed sectional curvature of g0 is zero.

For completeness, the minimum-point curvature identity can be derived directly from the connection difference, avoiding an unsupported curvature sign convention. With the convention K(X,Y)=g(R(X,Y)Y,X), the two connections satisfy

`∇~_X Y = ∇_X Y + df(X)Y + df(Y)X − g0(X,Y)grad_0 f`.

At p the difference tensor itself vanishes. Differentiating it in ∇_X A(Y,Y)−∇_Y A(X,Y), with local g0-normal fields, and pairing with X gives exactly

`−Hess_0 f(X,X)−Hess_0 f(Y,Y)`.

The numerator of sectional curvature scales by e^(2f) and the denominator by e^(4f), so

`K~(span{X,Y}) = e^(−2f(p))[0−Hess_0 f(X,X)−Hess_0 f(Y,Y)] ≤ 0`.

Thus no smooth conformal factor, small or large, makes this product conformal class positively curved everywhere. The proof does not rule out nonconformal metrics, which are the essential remaining domain of the full problem.

## 76: a nonzero formal jet defeats a first-order shortcut

Take n=4 and A=diag(−3,−1,1,3). Then trace A=0, S=tr(A²)=20 and det A=9. Specify a fully symmetric tensor T by

`T111=2√5, T122=T212=T221=−4√5, T133=T313=T331=2√5`,

with all other entries zero. Full symmetry imposes the first-order Codazzi symmetry. The differentiated mean-curvature constraint is Σ_i T_iik=0, and the differentiated constant-S constraint is Σ_i λ_iT_iik=0. Both hold for each k: for k=1 the coefficient sums are 2−4+2=0 and (−3)2+(−1)(−4)+(1)2=0, and the other k sums vanish.

The norm is `5(2²+3·(−4)²+3·2²)=320`, equal to S(S−n), as required by the scalar Simons identity for a minimal hypersurface with constant S. The cofactors of A are (−3,−9,9,3), however, giving

`∂1(det A)=[(−3)2+(−9)(−4)+9·2]√5=48√5`,

and zero for the other three derivatives. Thus the listed necessary algebraic/first-derivative constraints do not force constant determinant. The Gauss equation can separately define an algebraic curvature tensor from A, but no claim is made that this jet prolongs to a local immersed hypersurface. Higher derivatives, compatibility, and global compact realization are unresolved. In particular this is not a counterexample to Chern's conjecture.

## 77: a complete exact calculation for a symmetric polytope family

Let P=conv(±e1,…,±e4), v=t(1,1,1,1), and K_t=conv(P,±v), with t≥1/2. The cross-polytope P has volume 2/3. Its facets are indexed by sign vectors σ and lie on σ·x=1. The simplex obtained by joining such a facet to zero has volume 1/24; adjoining v beyond that facet adds volume (σ·v−1)/24 when σ·v>1.

For t≥1/2, the facets visible from v have all four signs positive, contributing 4t−1, or exactly three positive signs, contributing 2t−1 for each of four facets. Boundary equality at t=1/2 contributes zero. The point −v contributes the symmetric additions. These additions account for all of K_t: any convex combination using v and −v can cancel their smaller coefficient into a multiple of zero, which lies in P, leaving a point in conv(P,v) or conv(P,−v). Their interiors outside P are disjoint. Hence

`Vol(K_t)=2/3+2[(4t−1)+4(2t−1)]/24=t+1/4`.

The polar is

`K_t°={y: |y_i|≤1 and |y1+y2+y3+y4|≤1/t}`.

Set y_i=2u_i−1 and q=1/(2t), so 0<q≤1. This clips the unit four-cube to `2−q≤Σu_i≤2+q`, with Jacobian 16. By inclusion–exclusion, the volume below Σu_i=s for 1≤s≤2 is `(s⁴−4(s−1)⁴)/24`. Symmetry about sum two therefore gives

`Vol(K_t°)=16[1−((2−q)⁴−4(1−q)⁴)/12]`

`=32/(3t)−4/(3t³)+1/(4t⁴)`.

Multiplying and subtracting the four-dimensional Mahler target 4⁴/4!=32/3 yields

`Vol(K_t)Vol(K_t°)−32/3=(128t³−64t²−4t+3)/(48t⁴)`.

With u=2t−1≥0 the numerator is `16u³+32u²+14u+1`, strictly positive. This proves the desired inequality throughout this specific family. The script also checks exact rational samples, including t=1 where the product is 575/48 and the gap is 21/16. This is a restricted derivation, with no assertion that the family exhausts extremizers or that the calculation is new.

## 78–80: explicit obstruction and geometric certificates

For item 78, in A=C[x,y,z]/(xy−z²+1) set δ(x)=0,δ(y)=2z,δ(z)=x and ε(x)=2z,ε(y)=0,ε(z)=y. Direct differentiation shows both annihilate the relation. Their saved finite generator chains end at zero, which implies local nilpotence on every polynomial by the Leibniz rule and finite generation. But δ(x)=0 while ε(x)=2z≠0. Thus a coordinate singled out by one such derivation is not necessarily invariant under another. This defeats that proposed shortcut, not affine-space cancellation.

For item 79, the projective conic yz−x²=0 is smooth because its gradient (−2x,z,y) cannot vanish at a projective point; a smooth plane conic is irreducible. The ten specified points are distinct and lie on it. Blowing up these points gives strict-transform class C=2H−ΣEi, with intersection rules H²=1, H·Ei=0, Ei·Ej=−δij. Consequently C²=−6 and K·C=4. Adjunction gives g=0. The construction changes the surface if the number of points changes, so it cannot establish unbounded negativity on a fixed surface by varying that number.

For item 80, D4 is the set of integer vectors with even coordinate sum. The displayed basis has determinant two. No vector has norm one because a unit coordinate vector has odd sum; the 24 vectors ±ei±ej realize squared norm two. Balls of radius √2/2 have volume π²/8, giving density π²/16 after division by the covolume two.

Let L have basis (2b1,b2,b3,b4), so D4=L∪(b1+L) with cell covolume four. Move only the second coset to b1+se1+L. Both b1+se1 and −b1+se1 are inter-coset difference vectors because 2b1 belongs to L. Since b1=(1,−1,0,0), their squared norms are 2+2s+s² and 2−2s+s². Therefore the new minimum squared distance is at most 2−2|s|+s², strictly less than two for 0<|s|<2. There are still two centers per unchanged cell, so the admissible ball radius decreases and the density cannot improve along this path. This explicit control agrees with already established [periodic extremality](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.2013); it is not a global packing proof or a search of arbitrary finite-amplitude deformations.
